One component, four openings (shown with A)
1 · Bulk bar — Change slot
Opens upward from the bar. Caption: "Move 3 bills to". Confirm: "Move to 12:30".
2 · Panel — slot chip
Opens downward under the chip. Caption: "Move this bill to". Confirm: "Move".
3 · Rail card — pick slot
Caption: "Send to floor on". Confirm: "Release to 12:30" — this one both slots and releases.
4 · Panel — Release a held bill
Caption: "Release to". Confirm: "Release to 12:30". No day pre-selected — a held bill has no slot to inherit.
What changed, in all three options
  • Unselected days lose their grey fill. Grey fill on everything means nothing stands out. Now only the chosen day is painted.
  • The selected day is near-black, not teal. Teal moves to the confirm button — the only control that actually writes to the bill. One teal per surface, and it lands on the thing that matters.
  • "WINDOW" in caps is gone. Four times sitting under six dates explains itself.
  • "Jul" drops off each tile. It was repeated six times to say one thing. The month lives in the caption line if the range ever crosses over.
  • No "Today" label. Every tile is the same height and shape. The strip always starts at today and today is selected by default, so the first tile is today by definition — a label would only make one tile taller than the rest.
  • Same card everywhere. Only the caption line and the confirm button's wording change per opening.
My pick: A. "Sharp" is really about edges, and A is the only one with a crisp outer frame — it reads as one deliberate control rather than a scatter of tiles. B is quieter but the days stop looking tappable, which matters on a depot screen where nobody reads instructions. C is clear but puts the grey weight straight back.
