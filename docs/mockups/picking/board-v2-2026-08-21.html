Picking — Supervisor Board v2

Static review mockup · 2026-08-21, detail screen revised 2026-08-22 · docs/mockups/picking/board-v2-2026-08-21.html · nothing here is wired to code or the database

Five changes to the supervisor face of /picking, drawn from the findings in docs/prompts/drafts/code-discovery-2026-08-21-picking-board-v2.md. Every screen below is 390px wide, the real phone width the floor uses, and every colour, size and weight is taken from the live components — nothing new was designed.

What is on this page

  1. Route filter sheet — fixed. Only routes that actually have a bill under the selected type. No more "Vapi 0". Shown twice: with Local selected, and with UPC selected — where Adajan appears again, at 3, because one route name can sit under two delivery types. (An optional third frame printing the bay after each route name was drawn and dropped — the bay is not on this sheet.)
  2. The card prints the ROUTE, not the area. Current and new side by side, on the same bill, plus a long-route case so you can see what truncates. The detail-screen header moves with it (2b).
  3. The detail header, rebuilt — and THE BAND (revised 2026-08-22). The header goes to two rows so the dealer name gets a whole line; the route leaves its subtitle; the three flag pills become 26px symbols; the icons grow to 22px in 44px targets (2b). route_master.bayNumber — which bay the route's material is stacked at — now rides a full-width band under that header, carrying bay · route · the recording triangle on one strip, on both faces (2c, and 2d for the picker's). Still on the open bill only: not on a card, not in the route sheet, not on the Picking-tab list. Two treatments were tried and dropped — a bay coloured by delivery type (a bay is a physical stack; bay 1 serves Local and Upcountry both) and the 44px amber circle that shipped on 08-21 and lasted a day. A null bay drops the number and the rule; a null route prints Unmatched and the band stays.
  4. Assign-to-picker sheet — one design, three states. A tall sheet of two-up picker cards, each with a green/purple status dot borrowed from Floor Control. It opens showing free pickers only; one grey row reveals the rest. Shown collapsed, expanded, and in the case that decides whether collapsing is safe at all — nobody free.
  5. Picking tab becomes picker-first — three levels. Level 1 the pickers (two designs), level 2 that man's bills, level 3 the bill itself. Plus the empty state.
  6. Search widened to five fields — picker name · customer name · route · area · OBD. Four demo queries, all on the Picking tab, because that is where the new picker-first list makes the answer visible.
What is NOT changing. The Done tab keeps its two bands, its own picker dropdown and its current cards — untouched. The swipe-between-bills gesture is unchanged, including its four tuning constants — it still pages a flat list of bills, it just pages one picker's bills now. The Picking tab badge stays a BILL count, not a picker count, on every screen below. And a bill with no bay number shows nothing at all — no circle, no chip, no dash, no empty placeholder; its card and its header render exactly as they do today (HAND and No Route have no bay, and both are on this page proving it).

The picker's own "My Picks" board is otherwise untouched — screen 2d is the one place it appears, because the bay is his information before it is anybody else's.

1 · Route filter sheet — only routes that have a bill under this type

Today the list of routes is built once from every waiting bill and never narrowed by the type pills, while the counts beside them are. That is why "Vapi 0" sits in the list with Local selected. Both sides now come from the same filtered set, so a route with nothing under the current type simply is not offered. The subtitle changes with it: the old line, "counts reflect the current Type filter", stops being what the sheet does.

2b · The detail-screen header — rebuilt 2026-08-22

Four changes, one idea: give the dealer name the whole line, and move everything that was fighting it for space somewhere it fits.

  • The route leaves the subtitle. It moves to the band below, so it appears exactly once on the screen. The subtitle is now just {obdNumber} · {window}. The ?? "Unmatched" that used to sit here did not vanish with it — the band prints "Unmatched" on its route side when the route is null, which is why removing it here loses nothing.
  • The three flag pills become symbols — ★ ⚡ 🎨, a 26px coloured circle each. The words they carried live on in the aria-label and the title, so a screen reader still announces them and a long press still shows them. Supervisor face only.
  • The chips get their own line, indented 44px to sit under the title. That is what frees row 1 entirely for the name.
  • The icons grow — glyph 17 → 22px in a 44px tap target, gap 0 so the pair reads as one cluster. The back tile goes 32 → 38px, radius 10, white 16%. Right padding drops to 6px.

Unchanged The SMU badge and the duplicate-SO tag keep their exact treatment — the SMU badge carries its meaning in its colour (UI §1209), which a frosted overlay or a symbol swap would erase. The chip row's five-way render guard keeps all five of its conditions; two of them are recorded bug-fixes.

2c · The band — bay, route and the recording triangle on one strip

A picker opens a bill and needs one thing before he moves: which bay do I walk to. The answer is now a full-width white strip directly under the header, carrying the bay number in amber, a rule, the route name, and the findings triangle at its right end. Both faces, same strip, same position.

Superseded The 44px amber circle is gone — it lasted one day (2026-08-21 → 08-22). It worked, but it was competing for the header's icon cluster with the search, the kebab and the triangle, and it could only ever show the NUMBER. A bay number with no route beside it is a number you have to trust; on the band the two sit on one line, so the bay can never be read against the wrong route. The earlier type-coloured bay is gone too, and for the original reason: a bay is a physical stack, and bay 1 serves Local and Upcountry routes both, so colouring it by type implied a split that does not exist.

#d97706, and the hex is bound to the background. The band was a dark #0a5049 teal for part of one day, with the number at #fbbf24; white ground needs the darker amber, because a light amber on white is a smudge at arm's length and being readable from across a warehouse is the only thing this number is for. Change the band's background and you change this hex with it. It is still the only amber on the detail screen — the cards keep their amber age badge (CLAUDE_UI.md §62.1) and amber urgent bolt, and no bay object has ever gone on a card.

The seam is a border now, not an inset shadow. A shadow seam is what two DARK surfaces need; white meeting white needs a line, and a shadow there reads as a smudge rather than a division. It changed with the background, for the same reason the amber did.

The triangle ALWAYS lives on the band, on both faces, and never returns to the header. A control that moves between two homes depending on the bill is worse than a band that is occasionally sparse. What it DOES is unchanged: it toggles one screen-level mode, writes nothing, and makes the line rows tappable. Its gate is unchanged too — the supervisor gets it on a bill he is checking, the picker on a bill he can still act on.

Why the band does not collapse on an unmatched bill. The route left the header subtitle in this same change. If the band ALSO dropped out when there is no route, the word "Unmatched" would disappear from the screen entirely — and "this bill's dealer resolved to nothing" is exactly the thing a supervisor must not have to infer from an absence. So a null route prints "Unmatched" on the route side and the band stays. What a null bay drops is only the number and the rule.

Why 30px and 800 on the number. CLAUDE_UI.md §60's "nothing is 700" governs mobile CARDS, where competing weights make a list read dense. This is a single figure on a strip of its own, read at arm's length from a moving trolley, and it is the one number on the screen that decides where the picker physically walks.

2d · The picker's own board — he is the man who walks to the bay

Everything above is the supervisor's face. The band matters more on the picker's "My Picks" board, because he is the one carrying the trolley: same band, same position, same rules. His CARD gets the route swap and nothing else — no bay object, here or anywhere. His card keeps its own divergence, the articleTag pack summary on the where-line, which the supervisor card dropped.

Two things differ on his header, and only two. His chip row carries the duplicate tag and the SMU badge and nothing else — the ★ ⚡ 🎨 symbols were deliberately NOT added here, because this header has never carried Key dealer / Urgent / Tint and gaining them was not part of this change. And his header has no right-hand icons at all: this face has no search and no kebab, and the triangle — the one control that was up there — is on the band now. So row 1 is back plus the name, and the name gets all of it.

Changed His article row gains the volume, which used to sit on the right beside the bill arrows. Left is now packs · litres — the supervisor's exact treatment — and the right holds the pager alone. Three separate things on the right is what made that strip read as one undifferentiated slab (CLAUDE_PICKING.md §7's deferred note).

2e · The Done tab's CHECKED card — rebuilt

The lower band of the Done tab: bills the supervisor has already approved. Settled history, and it should look it. Four changes, and one hard rule about the band above it.

  • The volume arrives. This was the only card on the board that showed a bill without ever saying how much was in it. It takes the right end of the where-row.
  • The picker's name leaves that slot and moves into the footer sentence, where a verb anchors it. Floating on the right it was a name attached to nothing.
  • The SMU loses its pill and rides the caption as a bare indigo number. SmuBadge itself is untouched — every other card still shows the pill.
  • A green footer in the shelf's position, so the card's silhouette is unchanged and only the colour says "settled".

Hard rule The NEEDS-CHECK band never gets this footer. Those bills still want an action from the supervisor; these are finished. Green on both would erase the one difference the tab exists to show, and it would become a list of things that all look done.

The sentence wraps, it does not truncate. Two variable-length names on one line is real overflow — but this footer records who is accountable, and half a checker's name records nobody. It wraps to a second line and the card grows; the time stays pinned to the first line so it is never the piece that moves. A null checker degrades to "✓ Picked {name}" with the clause dropped — never "checked null", and never a suppressed footer.

3 · Assign-to-picker sheet

One design. The sheet opens tall — roughly the whole phone, grab handle on top, header pinned, the grid scrolling under it — so the supervisor sees every free picker without a scroll. Pickers are two-up cards, and each carries a status dot taken from Floor Control: green = Free · purple = Picking. A free card says just "Free"; a busy card says how many bills are actually in his hands — still at pick_assigned, any date. A bill he marks done frees him immediately. Today the chip reads "5 jobs", which counts everything handed to him since midnight including the ones he already finished, so a man with empty hands can read as the busiest on the floor.

Default Free pickers only. Busy pickers are not rendered until the supervisor asks for them — the sheet exists to answer "who can take this", and four names that cannot are noise. One full-width row at the bottom opens the rest. It is grey, not teal: it is navigation, not the action this sheet is for.

This is the frame that decides whether collapsing is safe. With nobody free the sheet has no default content, so it drops the collapse entirely: every picker, immediately, one quiet line saying why, and no show-all row — there is nothing left to show. A sheet that opened empty with only a grey row on it would read as broken at the exact moment the floor is busiest.

What the collapse costs. One extra tap, and only for a supervisor who wants a specific busy man — to stack a second bill on the picker already walking that aisle, or to reassign work off someone. That is a real habit on a small floor, and this design makes it slower. The trade is that the common case, "hand this to whoever is free", loses the four names that cannot take it. If the floor turns out to reach past the row often, the honest fix is to open expanded and keep the ordering, not to shrink the tap target.

5 · Search — one box, five fields

The box already exists and already works; it just only looks at two of these five today. All five, plainly: picker name · customer name · route · area · OBD. Customer name and OBD are the two it already matches; picker name, route and area are new — and all five are already on every row of the payload, so none of this needs a new query. The placeholder changes from "Search customer or OBD…" to "Search picker, customer, route, area or OBD…", which names every one of them rather than leaving the floor to discover the rest by accident.

Decision At level 1, search filters to the PICKERS WHO HAVE A MATCHING BILL — and drilling in shows only the bills that matched. That is the reading these four frames show, and it is worth agreeing before it is built. The alternative — leave the picker list whole and only narrow it at level 2 — is defensible (you keep the whole floor in view) but it makes searching feel like it did nothing. The card's "2 of 3 bills" line is what stops the narrowing from being invisible.

Notes for whoever builds this

  • Nothing here needs a new query, a new column or a migration. Route, area, picker name and picker id are all already on every row of the picking payload; the route filter and the search both run on rows the board has already loaded.
  • The one server change is the assign sheet's count. The route already returns a pending figure — it is just not the number the chip renders, and it is fenced to a UTC day, which is why a carry-over bill can leave a busy picker reading Free. Both are described in the discovery report's Q3.
  • The roster is fetched once, on mount. That was fine for a list of names and is not fine for a live count — every number in screen 3 goes stale the moment the supervisor assigns anything. Refreshing it when the sheet opens is the small piece of plumbing this design assumes.
  • The route sheet must also decide about upcoming bills. The list and the counts both include locked, future-dated bills today, while the strip above the control counts only what is due. Filtering by type alone leaves that mismatch in place.
  • The elapsed pill on a picker card is the oldest bill in his hands. Every frame here uses that reading. It is not the only possible one — an average, or the count over 30m, would both be defensible — but one bill's clock is the one a supervisor can act on.
